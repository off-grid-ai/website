## Where they differ

MacWhisper is a transcription tool. You record a call or dictate, and you get a transcript you can summarise, with local models or your own API keys.

OGAD treats the transcript as a start, not the end product:

- **The call becomes notes.** A local text model writes the summary, the decisions and who owes what.
- **The notes join your memory.** Ask "what did Priya promise last month?" and get an answer from calls, mail and screens together.
- **Follow-ups turn into drafts.** A reply in Gmail or an issue in Linear, Jira or Notion, sent only when you say yes.
- **It works beyond the Mac.** The same app on Windows and Linux, so a mixed team or a second PC isn't left out.

## How a call works in OGAD

1. Join a Zoom, Teams or Google Meet call. OGAD sees it and records two tracks: the call's audio and your microphone.
2. For a meeting in a room, press **Record meeting**.
3. Press **Stop**. A local Whisper model writes the transcript, and a local text model writes the summary.

## When MacWhisper is the better pick

MacWhisper is focused and easy to start with. It lets you switch between local and remote models, and it has a free version. If you work only on a Mac and want a transcript tool, not an assistant, MacWhisper at €64 is a fair deal.

If you use Windows or Linux, or want your calls to feed a memory and a follow-up list, use OGAD.
