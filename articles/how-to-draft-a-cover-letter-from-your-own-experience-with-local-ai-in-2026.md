## Start with evidence, not a blank page

The hardest part is often deciding what to include. A local model can help select two or three relevant examples and organise them into a short letter. It should not create qualifications, responsibilities, or results that you do not have.

Suppose you are applying for a project coordinator role after working in a small agency. You have experience tracking deliverables, preparing client updates, and resolving missing inputs. The letter should explain those connections. It should not claim that you led a department simply because the job description asks for leadership.

Your first useful result is a match table between the role's needs and your evidence.

## Prepare three short inputs

Use the job description, your current CV, and a note of relevant examples. Save a copy of the job description because the page may change or disappear.

For each example, record the task, your action, and the result you can support. If a result has no verified number, describe the outcome without a number. “Prepared a weekly client update” is better than an invented claim about saving hours.

| Input | What to check |
|---|---|
| Job description | Current role, required skills, application instructions |
| CV | Accurate dates, titles, qualifications, and contact details |
| Experience note | Your contribution and supportable outcomes |

Do not include reference contact details or other people's private information unless needed and appropriate. You can use a working copy with less personal detail while drafting.

## Use a local chat or a small project

Install OGAD and download a local text model. For a short application, paste the three inputs into a fresh chat in manageable sections. For longer material, use a dedicated project.

Open **Projects > New project**, then **Knowledge & settings > Knowledge base > Add files**. Add readable PDF, DOCX, TXT, or Markdown files, wait for indexing, and start **Chats > New chat** inside the project. If **Include captured memory** is available, turn it off for this source-only task and save.

Projects and document chat are core features. Complete the initial model and search downloads before working offline. Keep a local model selected; remote models and external tools have separate network behavior.

The [document extractor](https://github.com/off-grid-ai/OGAD/blob/v0.0.51/src/main/rag/extractors.ts) reads available text. If the CV is an image-only PDF, use a checked text version rather than assuming every detail was imported.

## Build the match table

Ask:

> Compare the job description with my CV and experience notes. List the three most relevant requirements, the evidence I actually have for each, and any gap. Name the source. Do not infer a qualification or level of responsibility that is not stated. Keep desirable criteria separate from mandatory ones.

Check the table yourself. A mention of a tool is not proof of advanced expertise. A team result does not establish that you were the person responsible for it.

For the coordinator example, tracking deliverables and preparing updates may be strong matches. If the role requires budget ownership and you have only helped record expenses, describe that accurately rather than stretching the claim.

## Choose the letter's main point

Write one sentence explaining why this role fits your experience. Then choose two examples that support it. This gives the draft a clear direction.

Ask for a short first version:

> Draft a cover letter using only the checked match table below. Open with the role and my relevant experience. Use two concrete examples. Explain why this work interests me using the reasons I supplied. Do not invent enthusiasm, company knowledge, achievements, or qualifications. Keep the tone direct and the letter under 350 words.

If you have not supplied a reason for applying, write one yourself. Generic claims about being “passionate about innovation” do little to explain your choice.

## Make the examples specific without making them long

Each example needs enough detail to be credible: what you worked on, what you did, and why that matters for the role.

For instance, a checked fact might become: “In my agency role, I maintained the delivery tracker and prepared weekly client updates, which helped the team see missing inputs before review meetings.” Only use the final clause if your experience supports it.

Ask the model to remove claims that have no evidence:

> Highlight any sentence that adds a fact, result, or motivation not present in my checked notes. Suggest a shorter version that uses only supported information.

Treat that as another review aid, not a guarantee. Read the source yourself for important claims.

## Edit it into your own voice

Read the letter aloud. Replace words you would not use in an interview. Remove repeated statements of confidence and any sentence that could be sent unchanged to every employer.

Keep the employer's name, role title, and recipient correct. Do not ask the model to invent a hiring manager. If the application instructions specify a format or a question to answer, follow those instructions directly.

A cover letter should complement the CV. It does not need to repeat every position in chronological order.

## Check the final application outside the chat

Copy the reviewed letter into your document tool or application form. Check the exported file, not just the chat text. Confirm that paragraphs, contact details, and any required reference number survived the transfer.

Use your editor's word count if there is a limit. Verify dates and qualifications against your records. Remove comments or working notes from the version you submit.

This workflow creates a draft. It does not submit an application or predict how a recruiter will evaluate it.

## Reuse the evidence, not the whole letter

Keep your checked experience notes for the next application. For each new role, rebuild the match table and choose the examples that fit. That is more useful than changing the company name in a generic letter.
