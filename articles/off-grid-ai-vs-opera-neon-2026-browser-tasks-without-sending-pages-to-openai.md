## How a browser task runs on your computer

1. Install the [Off Grid AI extension](/browser-extension/) in Chrome and keep OGAD running.
2. In the extension, open **Settings > Desktop** and choose **Pair with desktop**. Check that the six words match, then approve in OGAD.
3. Turn on **Agent** and describe the result you want, for example a table of three ticket statuses with links.
4. Watch the plan and each step in OGAD. Guide it if it picks the wrong part of the page, or press **Stop**.
5. When the task wants to send, create or delete something, OGAD shows the action. You approve it or reject it.

The extension refuses one-time codes, card numbers and CAPTCHAs in code. Sign-ins come from your Vault or from you. Opera says Neon keeps passwords and cookies on your device too. The difference is the page itself: in OGAD, a local model reads it.

## When Opera Neon is the better pick

Neon gives you top cloud models in one subscription, with several agents for agendas, browsing and research. It also includes Opera's ad blocker and free VPN. And it runs on any laptop, because the planning happens in the cloud. OGAD needs a computer that can run a local model. Most Apple silicon Macs and recent PCs can, and the app shows which models fit.

If you want a new browser with frontier models and the pages you visit are public, Neon is a fair choice. If your browser holds client, legal or deal pages, keep the model on your own machine. Off Grid AI's extension is on Chrome today; Edge, Brave, Arc and Firefox have a waitlist.
