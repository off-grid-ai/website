## 1. Off Grid AI

**What it is.** Off Grid AI Mobile (OGAM) for iPhone and Android, and Off Grid AI Desktop (OGAD) for Mac (Apple silicon), Windows and Linux. Both run open GGUF models such as Qwen 3.5, Llama, Gemma and Phi on the device. The phone makes images with Stable Diffusion; the computer uses its GPU. Projects answer from your files and cite the passage. The desktop app also serves an OpenAI-compatible API at `127.0.0.1:7878`, for chat, images and speech.

**Best for.** One private assistant on every device you own, with no account.

**Price.** Free. Pro is optional at $79.99 once (limited time, regular $119) or $7.99 a month, for up to 5 devices. On the phone it adds voice mode, drafts you approve and Sync. On the computer it adds Memory, Meetings, Actions, God, Vault and more.

**The catch.** The Mac build is for Apple silicon only. Model speed depends on your device; the app shows which models fit. An iPhone 15 Pro or an 8 GB Android flagship runs Qwen 3.5 4B well.

## 2. LM Studio

**What it is.** A strong desktop app for running local models, with llama.cpp and MLX and "OpenAI-like endpoints".

**Best for.** MLX on a Mac, Windows on ARM, and a headless build for servers and CI.

**Price.** Free for local models. Bionic+ is $20 a month and Pro $100 a month for its cloud agent.

**The catch.** No phone app and no image generation listed. See [Off Grid AI vs LM Studio](/articles/off-grid-ai-vs-lm-studio-2026-local-models-plus-memory-and-actions-for-79-once/).

## 3. Jan

**What it is.** A free, open-source (AGPL-3.0) local chat app with an OpenAI-compatible API and MCP.

**Best for.** Simple local chat on any computer.

**Price.** Free.

**The catch.** Desktop only, and no images or voice listed. See [Off Grid AI vs Jan](/articles/off-grid-ai-vs-jan-2026-local-ai-that-makes-images-talks-back-and-runs-on-your-phone/).

## 4. Ollama

**What it is.** A small, MIT-licensed model runtime with a CLI and a simple app. Many tools use it as a backend.

**Best for.** Developers building their own app on a local runtime.

**Price.** Free on your own hardware.

**The catch.** Image generation is experimental and macOS only, and no phone app is listed. See [Off Grid AI vs Ollama](/articles/off-grid-ai-vs-ollama-2026-one-local-api-for-chat-images-and-speech-plus-an-app/).

## 5. PocketPal

**What it is.** A free, open-source offline chat app for iOS and Android, with Pals, read-aloud and benchmarks.

**Best for.** Offline chat on a phone, nothing more.

**Price.** Free.

**The catch.** No computer app, no images and no file answers listed. See [Off Grid AI vs PocketPal](/articles/off-grid-ai-vs-pocketpal-2026-the-offline-phone-ai-that-also-makes-images/).

## 6. AnythingLLM

**What it is.** Desktop apps, a Docker server, a team cloud and an Android app for chatting with your documents.

**Best for.** Teams that want a document chat server.

**Price.** Core apps free.

**The catch.** No iPhone app, and no image generation listed. See [Off Grid AI vs AnythingLLM](/articles/off-grid-ai-vs-anythingllm-2026-private-ai-on-your-iphone-with-images-and-sync/).

## 7. Private LLM

**What it is.** A paid on-device chat app for iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple Vision.

**Best for.** Apple-only households who want a one-time buy.

**Price.** $4.99 once on the US App Store.

**The catch.** No Android, Windows or Linux, and its site doesn't say it is open source. See [Off Grid AI vs Private LLM](/articles/off-grid-ai-vs-private-llm-2026-free-open-source-and-on-android-and-windows-too/).

## 8. Google AI Edge Gallery

**What it is.** Google's showcase of on-device AI, running LiteRT models such as Gemma, on Android, iOS and a macOS preview.

**Best for.** Trying Google's on-device models.

**Price.** Open source under Apache-2.0.

**The catch.** Its README calls it an "experimental Beta release", and it runs LiteRT models, not GGUF. See [Off Grid AI vs Google AI Edge Gallery](/articles/off-grid-ai-vs-google-ai-edge-gallery-2026-stable-offline-ai-with-any-gguf-model/).

## 9. GPT4All

**What it is.** One of the first local chat apps, MIT licensed, for Windows, macOS and Linux.

**Best for.** Existing users with a setup that works.

**Price.** Open source.

**The catch.** Its latest GitHub release is v3.10.0, from 25 February 2025. See [Off Grid AI vs GPT4All](/articles/off-grid-ai-vs-gpt4all-2026-the-local-ai-app-that-still-ships-every-week/).

Also worth knowing: [Open WebUI](/articles/off-grid-ai-vs-open-webui-2026-private-chatgpt-on-your-own-device-no-server-to-run/) is a good interface, but it needs Ollama or another model server behind it.
