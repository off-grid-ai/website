## How a browser task runs on your computer

1. Install the [Off Grid AI extension](/browser-extension/) in Chrome and keep OGAD running.
2. In the extension, open **Settings > Desktop** and choose **Pair with desktop**. Check that the six words match, then approve in OGAD.
3. Turn on **Agent** and ask for a result you can check, such as a table with source links.
4. Watch the plan and each step in OGAD. Correct it if it reads the wrong section, or press **Stop**.
5. When the task wants to send, create or delete something, OGAD shows the action. You approve it or reject it.

The task uses the sessions you are already signed in to. The extension refuses one-time codes, card numbers and CAPTCHAs in code. For a full walk-through, see [how to automate tasks in your signed-in browser with local AI](/articles/how-to-automate-tasks-in-your-signed-in-browser-with-local-ai-in-2026/).

## When Fellou is the better pick

Fellou is built for long research runs. Deep Search pulls many sources into a report, and tasks can run in a background workspace while you browse. It schedules recurring tasks, and its Eko framework lets developers build custom agents. If your work is mostly public-web research and you want a browser made for it, Fellou is worth a try.

OGAD needs a computer that can run a local model. Most Apple silicon Macs and recent PCs can, and the app shows which models fit. A local model can be slower than a large cloud model on big research jobs. If your tasks touch client portals, internal tools or private accounts, keep the model on your own machine.
