## How an action works in Off Grid AI

1. You ask, or God notices something in your day. For example: "Reply to Sam and confirm Friday."
2. A local model reads the thread and your notes, then drafts the reply with sources.
3. The draft goes to **Actions** as a pending approval. Nothing is sent.
4. You read it. You can open it in chat to change it, then approve or reject it.
5. If you approve, OGAD sends it through your Gmail. The approval is written to a log you can open later.

Actions today work through Gmail, Linear, Jira and Notion. Connectors use MCP, so you can add others. The guide [Connector support in Off Grid AI Desktop](/articles/connector-support-in-off-grid-ai-desktop-private-approval-gated-integrations/) shows the fetch, reason and approve steps in detail.

## Where your data goes

With local models selected, OGAD reads and reasons on your computer. Your memory never goes to a server we run, because we run none.

Sending mail through Gmail still uses Gmail. Creating a Notion page still uses Notion. Those services see what you send them, as they always do. If you pick a remote model, the context for that answer goes to that provider.

## When Meta Muse is the better pick

Be fair to yourself. Muse is free for most uses, and it needs no computer at all. You can use it on a phone, on the web, or by message in WhatsApp. OGAD's agent runs on a computer: Mac (Apple silicon), Windows or Linux.

Muse also shops for you. Meta's launch post lists purchases among the actions Muse checks with you first. OGAD does not make purchases for you.

Meta also says it plans a Muse Confidential VM, encrypted with a key only you hold. If that ships, the cloud side gets stronger. Today, the agent, your files and your logins sit on a machine Meta runs.

If you want a free helper for errands and shopping, Muse is a fair choice. If you want your work context, meetings and mail on your own machine, with a log of every action you approved, run OGAD.
