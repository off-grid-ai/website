# Off Grid AI homepage: design and interaction audit

Date: 7 October 2026  
Page: http://localhost:4000/  
Scope: current homepage only. This report is for the agent that owns the current design. It is not a request to replace that design.

**Read the latest-build check at the end before acting.** The first 14 findings describe the first reviewed build. The other agent changed the page during the reviews. The addenda record new faults, fixes, and review limits. The latest check covers build `dd371b07402d`. Do not treat every first-pass finding as an open fault in that build.

## Verdict

The page has a clear direction now. The terminal type, emerald accent, product window, chapter dial, and horizontal card rails form a stronger whole. Keep that direction.

It still falls below the requested standard for a premium consumer website. The largest gap is the link between appearance and behavior. Some controls promise an action that they do not support. On mobile and tablet, the main tour loses its controls and its readable product detail. More effects will not resolve these faults.

The goal is a page that feels alive **and under the visitor's control**. Each action must give clear feedback, useful content, and a stable result. Motion must help the visitor see that result. It must not compete with it or replace it before the visitor has finished.

## Review scope and limits

- Manual browser review at 1440 × 900 desktop, 1024 × 768 tablet landscape, 768 × 1024 tablet portrait, 390 × 844 phone, and 320 × 740 small phone.
- Light and dark themes were viewed on desktop, phone, and tablet portrait. Tablet landscape and the small phone were viewed in light mode.
- Reviewed the hero, scroll transition, chapter selection, autoplay control, mobile navigation, privacy comparison, pricing, related offers, download section, and footer.
- Used screenshots, rendered DOM measurements, and focused source reads. No automated tests, build, or E2E suite was run. No website code was changed.
- Browser sizes are not physical devices. Touch hardware, mobile Safari, assistive technology, reduced-motion rendering, network performance, and checkout completion remain unverified.
- Source references point to the files as read during this review. Another agent may change them after this report.

## Keep these parts

1. **The product window as the main object.** It gives the page a clear center and a direct link to the product.
2. **The scroll transition from headline to tour.** This is a useful transition, with a purpose beyond decoration. Repair its narrow-screen geometry.
3. **The dial as a desktop tour control.** It can give the site a distinct identity. It needs better labels and a complete mobile alternative.
4. **Horizontal rails for related cards on phones.** Keep the short vertical flow. Add clear card choices and touch controls.
5. **The restrained visual system.** Menlo, neutral surfaces, and emerald can support depth and motion without a second accent.
6. **Named library primitives.** The build imports pinned SmoothUI, Motion Primitives, Magic UI, Aceternity, and Radix sources. These are real dependencies, not just visual references. Keep that source tracking.

## Priority 1: fix before release

### 1. The small-phone menu is outside the visible page

**Confirmed at 320 × 740.** The menu button starts at x = 285.8 px and ends at x = 333.8 px. The page content width was 305 px, including the browser's scrollbar allowance. Much of the menu control is clipped. The page does not provide horizontal scrolling to reach it.

This blocks a main route through the website. It is not a minor spacing fault.

**Required result:** the logo, theme control, Download action, and menu must fit without clipping. Give the menu a full touch area. Use the existing header controls; do not build a new navigation system.

Evidence: [small-phone header](homepage-2026-10-07/phone-320-header.jpg). Source: `preview-ui/page.jsx`, PageShell, lines 1072–1087; `preview-ui/page.css`, header sizing rules.

### 2. The mobile and tablet tour hides its main control

**Confirmed at 390 × 844 and 768 × 1024.** After scrolling into the tour, the dial is cropped and partly behind the sticky header. At 390 px, the dial wrapper was 139.5 px high while the wheel was 366 px high. The copy container began at y = -38 px. At tablet portrait size, the screenshot showed the title and hint but almost none of the dial choices.

The hint says “Swipe the dial or tap a chapter,” but the visitor cannot see a complete, clear control. The heading is not a substitute for navigation.

**Required result:** keep the dial on desktop. At narrow widths, use a named library carousel or tab primitive with visible chapter labels, a selected state, and previous/next controls. A partial arc can remain as a visual element only if navigation is fully usable without it. Keep the selected chapter, title, and product view in one visible group.

Evidence: [phone tour](homepage-2026-10-07/phone-tour-light.jpg), [tablet tour](homepage-2026-10-07/tablet-tour-light.jpg). Source: `preview-ui/page.jsx` lines 711–865; `preview-ui/page.css` lines 610, 632, 1126–1136.

### 3. The hero input promises typing but blocks it

**Confirmed in source and control presentation.** The hero shows a normal textbox with a send arrow. `CommandBar` prevents character entry, deletion, and paste. Clicking the field runs the current rotating suggestion. The search-region name explains this behavior, but the visible field does not.

A visitor who wants to try their own question gets a different action. This breaks the expected input behavior and weakens trust at the first interaction.

**Required result:** choose one clear contract. Either support text entry and explain the limited demo response, or use a library button/command chooser that visibly says “Play this example.” Keep the vanish effect only if it helps show the accepted action. Do not present a preset launcher as an editable AI input.

Source: `preview-ui/page.jsx` lines 678–703.

### 4. The privacy comparison presents a simulation as measured proof

**Confirmed in source.** The cloud copy count is `3 + (n % 4)`. “SENT 0 bytes” is fixed text. These values are not measurements. The heading “Type something private. Watch where it goes” makes the display appear to trace actual data flow.

The local panel also says “0 copies” while showing device-to-device transfer. Even if the intent is “zero third-party copies,” the current wording does not say that.

**Required result:** label the display as an illustration. Use public sample text. Describe third-party storage and local processing precisely. Remove invented copy counts, or clearly label them as sample values. Do not imply that the website checks the user's network traffic. Scope cloud claims to supported cases rather than one generic claim for all cloud services.

Source: `preview-ui/page.jsx` lines 919–943, 960–977. This is a trust and content fault, not a request for less motion.

### 5. Small touch controls make the rails difficult to use

**Measured at 390 px.** Carousel indicator buttons are 8 × 8 px. The autoplay control is 24 px high. Prompt chips are 35 px high. The theme button is 38 × 38 px and Download is 36 px high. These do not meet the brand's 44 px touch-area rule.

**Required result:** keep small dots visually, but put each inside a touch area of at least 44 × 44 px. Add named previous/next buttons or a clearly labeled tab row. Expand chip and header hit areas without making every element visually bulky. Leave enough separation between adjacent targets.

Source: `preview-ui/page.css` lines 1226–1229; `MobileRail` and PrivacySection in `preview-ui/page.jsx` lines 952–977. Carousel indicators are from Motion Primitives; improve the adapter or shared primitive, not a separate local control.

### 6. Product detail does not survive the responsive layout

**Visual finding across widths, strongest on phone and tablet portrait.** Full app screenshots shrink or crop into the product window. Sidebars and dense tables occupy most of the image. The text that proves the feature is too small to read. On tablet portrait, the right side of the desktop app view is cut off. On phones, a large vertical image still delivers little readable detail.

The product looks real, but the visitor has to infer what it does.

**Required result:** choose a deliberate crop for each chapter and each device class. Show one readable result first: a cited answer, a meeting decision, an approval, or a saved item. Use existing image, frame, carousel, and dialog primitives. An optional enlargement must be a visible action, not an assumed gesture. Do not make screenshot controls look live unless they are interactive.

Source: Shot at `preview-ui/page.jsx` lines 85–92; walkthrough scene and window rules in `preview-ui/page.css` lines 643–655 and 734–769.

## Priority 2: improve the quality of the experience

### 7. Seventeen icons create too much work for a new visitor

**Design finding.** The dial puts 17 chapters at the same navigation level. Most choices are small icons without persistent visible labels. Hover can help a mouse user, but cannot be the only way to understand a choice on touch or with a keyboard.

**Required result:** offer a short set of clear entry points, then let the visitor explore the rest. Group the current chapters into a few useful paths such as remember, act, create, and use across devices. Keep the underlying chapters. Show a visible label on selection and on focus, with an equivalent touch path. The visitor should know where a choice goes before activating it.

### 8. Automatic motion competes with user control

**Source finding.** Tour selection stops chapter autoplay, which is good. However, `useCycle` restarts scene content on a timer. The privacy example changes every seven seconds and replaces the submitted prompt with another sample. The headline, model strip, grid, text reveals, beams, and scene loops also have separate motion schedules.

**Required result:** after an explicit selection or submission, hold the result until the visitor changes it or chooses Resume. Pause automatic content when it is outside the viewport. Establish one motion hierarchy: immediate control feedback, a short transition, then readable content. Keep ambient movement below that hierarchy. Do not reward a click by taking its result away.

Source: `preview-ui/page.jsx` lines 95–99, 794–797, 963–965.

### 9. The keyboard handler captures page-scroll keys

**Source finding; keyboard rendering not fully reviewed.** While the tour is docked, a window-level handler prevents the default action for Up, Down, Left, and Right. It excludes inputs and textareas but does not require focus inside the dial. A visitor can lose normal arrow-key page scrolling while focused elsewhere.

**Required result:** scope chapter shortcuts to the focused tour control. Preserve normal page navigation elsewhere. Show the same useful preview on focus that a mouse user gets on hover. Verify this later with the user's permission.

Source: `preview-ui/page.jsx` lines 785–793.

### 10. The text fields have no accessible names

**Confirmed from rendered DOM.** The two visible text inputs have no label, `aria-label`, `aria-labelledby`, or native placeholder. The rotating sample is a separate paragraph. The parent search-region name does not name its textbox. Send buttons also appear unnamed in the browser accessibility snapshot.

**Required result:** add stable field names and send-button names through the existing primitive. Keep rotating sample text as optional help. A screen-reader user must know what the field does without relying on the animation.

Source: CommandBar and PrivacySection; the Aceternity input adapter. No screen-reader hardware session was performed.

### 11. Pricing should not animate through false prices

**Confirmed on entry, then checked after settling.** Lifetime starts at $110 and settles at $69 while its button says $69. Monthly starts at $0 and settles at $4.99. The final values agree; this is not a lasting data mismatch. It is a poor use of NumberTicker for a purchasing decision.

**Required result:** show the exact price immediately. Animate the card or highlight instead. On mobile and tablet portrait, name the three plan choices above the rail; three tiny dots do not explain that Free and Monthly are available. Keep the preferred lifetime card, but make comparison easy. Align card actions on desktop; their current vertical positions differ.

Source: `preview-ui/page.jsx` lines 1123–1133.

### 12. Spacing and visual depth need a stronger hierarchy

**Design finding.** At 768 × 1024, the gap between hero proof and product window is roughly 240 px. The desktop manifesto is about 1080 px high for one sentence at a 900 px viewport. The same grid and dot treatment repeats through much of the page. On light surfaces it competes with screenshot detail; in dark mode the product frame and content can blend together.

**Required result:** define space by purpose: headline to action, action to proof, proof to product. Reduce empty travel that reveals no new state. Give the product window a stronger surface separation and keep its background quiet. Use the grid at selected moments instead of as equal-weight texture behind nearly every section. Depth should make the active object easier to identify.

Evidence: [desktop dark hero](homepage-2026-10-07/desktop-hero-dark.jpg). Source: `preview-ui/page.css` walkthrough geometry and manifesto height rules.

### 13. Light-mode secondary text is too faint

**Measured example.** Hero proof uses `rgb(138, 138, 138)` at 11 px. Against white, this is about 3.45:1 contrast, below the brand's 4.5:1 text rule. Faint icon and hint styling adds to the weak hierarchy.

**Required result:** use a readable secondary-text token for meaningful proof, instructions, and plan terms. Reserve faint colors for decoration. Do not make useful information quieter than the grid behind it.

### 14. Claims and copy need one focused pass

**Confirmed in source.** Hero counts are hardcoded: 250,000 downloads, 3,400 GitHub stars, and 600 community. No provenance was established in this audit. “Your God knows your day” introduces unexplained product terminology. “No server” and “0 copies” need an exact scope. Model availability was not independently verified.

**Required result:** confirm the figures and their date, scope the privacy claims, and name unfamiliar features clearly. Keep copy short. Each chapter needs a clear outcome, not a phrase the visitor must decode. Do not remove useful explanation merely to reduce word count.

Source: `preview-ui/page.jsx` hero-proof values at line 813, WALK data, and privacy labels.

## Items that need further review

- **Theme position stability:** during one phone theme change in the privacy section, the heading moved from near the top of the view to much lower down. The cause was not isolated. Check whether active scene sizing or theme image changes affect scroll position. Do not treat the cause as confirmed.
- **Reduced motion:** MotionConfig and the model strip contain support, and tour autoplay checks the preference. CSS pulse animation, WordRotate's timer, scene-cycle timers, and privacy auto-advance still need a complete reduced-motion review. A global MotionConfig does not stop arbitrary timers or CSS animations.
- **Focus visibility:** the input removes its native focus ring. Inspect and provide a clear focus state at the form level if the primitive does not supply one. Complete a keyboard review of menu focus return and tour controls.
- **Real-device behavior:** check mobile Safari focus zoom with the 14 px input, dynamic browser bars, touch scrolling over the dial, and swipe direction handling. These are not confirmed device faults.
- **Load cost and smoothness:** no timing, frame-rate, or network measurements were made. The page uses several animated layers and both theme image variants. Do not claim that there is no added load time without measuring the production page.
- **Source ownership:** pinned primitive imports are present. The command launcher adds its own typing sequence and the tour adds pointer capture and selection logic. Review these adapters against the user's “no custom components” rule. Do not claim full compliance merely because the page has many library imports.

### Updated conclusion

The most serious fault is **loss of user control**, not a lack of animation. A choice can be replaced by a timer. Keyboard focus can move a carousel without updating its state. Hidden content remains available to keyboard navigation. These faults turn a lively page into an unreliable one.

For the requested level of quality, the page needs three layers to agree at all times:

1. What the visitor sees and expects.
2. What the control accepts and changes.
3. What remains selected, readable, and recoverable afterward.

The current page often handles the transition but not the third layer. Repair that before adding effects.

### 15. P1 — Approval replaces the user's decision

**Observed in the browser and confirmed in ActScene.** I reset the approval demo and selected **Edit first**. No editor opened. The result then became **Approve and send**. No real email was sent; the demo says that explicitly. The fault is the demonstration of control.

ActScene schedules `setAuto('send')` after 3200 ms. AIApproval is keyed by that value, so it is remounted as an approved result. The page does not pass the primitive's `onDecide` callback. A user choice cannot cancel the timer or open an edit view.

This directly contradicts “Nothing goes out without your yes.” Confetti rewards the timer, not the visitor's decision.

**Required result:** user action must stop the automatic replay. Approve produces a clearly labeled sample success. Edit opens an editable sample draft through an existing dialog or input primitive. Not now produces a stable deferred state. Reset is the only action that clears that choice. Automatic replay must be a distinct mode, with no fake decision attributed to the user.

**Manual completion check for the owner:** select each option separately; read the result without touching the page; confirm that no timer replaces it. Then reset. No automated check is requested here.

Evidence: [approval result after Edit first](homepage-2026-10-07/approval-overrides-choice.jpg). Source owner: `ActScene` in `preview-ui/page.jsx`. The same timer and missing callback were still present in the later source read.

### 16. P1 — Carousel focus and selection use different positions

**Observed at 390 × 844.** From the lifetime-plan button, Tab moved focus to the monthly-plan button. The browser brought Monthly into view, but the carousel still marked slide 2, Lifetime, as selected. Clicking slide 2 then left two cards partly visible. The overflow container had a native `scrollLeft` of 179 px in addition to the carousel's transform.

The later build adds named plan tabs and previous/next controls. Those improve discovery, but do not resolve this state fault. In `f8a62fc5160a`, the focused control was still Start Pro monthly while `aria-selected` remained true on Lifetime.

**Required result:** focused slide, visible slide, active tab, indicator, and carousel index must agree. Hidden slide controls must not cause a second, native scroll position. Either use a primitive with correct focus handling or extend the shared primitive. Do not patch this only by hiding the scrollbar.

**Manual steps:** open pricing on a phone-sized viewport; select Lifetime; focus its action; press Tab; inspect the selected tab; select Lifetime again; confirm that one complete card appears. Also inspect reverse Tab and focus within the other rails.

Evidence from the earlier build: [focus moves to Monthly](homepage-2026-10-07/mobile-rail-focus-desync.jpg), [split cards after returning](homepage-2026-10-07/mobile-rail-split-cards.jpg). Source owners: MobileRail and the pinned Motion Primitives CarouselContent/CarouselItem.

### 17. P1 — Landscape leaves too little space for the product

**Observed at 844 × 390.** The earlier build reduced the tour window to **47 px high**, almost only its toolbar. In `f8a62fc5160a`, the window improved to **153 px**, with a **109 px content area**. The screenshot was still a small, unreadable image inside a wide frame.

The responsive rule subtracts the title/control block from the viewport height and assigns the remainder to the product. A viewport with little height needs a different flow, not only a smaller remainder.

**Required result:** below a usable height, stop pinning the whole composition into one viewport. Let the selected chapter and a readable result occupy normal document flow. Preserve a compact chapter control. Do not let the toolbar become most of the product view.

Evidence: [earlier landscape](homepage-2026-10-07/landscape-empty-product-window.jpg), [updated landscape](homepage-2026-10-07/updated-landscape-product-detail.jpg). Source owners: Walkthrough geometry and the `.wt-window` rule based on `--copyH`.

### 18. P1 — Invisible hero controls remain in the focus order

**Confirmed from the rendered DOM while the tour was docked.** The hero had opacity 0, but no `inert` or `aria-hidden` state. Seven hero controls still had a non-negative tab index: its badge link, input, and five suggestions. `.off` disables pointer events; it does not remove keyboard access.

The reverse issue applies to tour controls before they become visible. A fade must not create invisible focus stops. This matters on tablets with keyboards as well as desktop.

**Required result:** during a transition, make only the visible, active control group available. Preserve a logical focus destination when the visitor explicitly moves from the hero to the tour. Do not move focus merely because someone scrolls.

Source owner: Walkthrough's hero and copy groups. The later source still used opacity and `.off` without an inert state on the hero.

### 19. P2 — The enlarged dot hit areas overlap

**Confirmed in the updated styles and rendered measurements.** The updated dots remain 8 px wide with 8 px between them. A pseudo-element extends each target by 18 px on each side. That gives a nominal 44 px area, but centers are only 16 px apart. Adjacent targets overlap by about 28 px.

A target-size fix must not make it unclear which target receives a tap. Enlarging a pseudo-element does not reserve layout space.

**Required result:** use separate 44 px button boxes with a small dot centered inside each. Give each dot a programmatic selected state, or use the named tabs as the only selection control. The pinned CarouselIndicator expresses selection through CSS classes and does not set `aria-current` or `aria-pressed`.

### 20. P2 — Monthly intent is lost at the next page

**Observed navigation and confirmed in source.** Start Pro monthly and Own Pro for $69 both go to `/pro/#buy`. The destination leads with a lifetime card and a lifetime action. Its form's normal submit action also chooses lifetime. Monthly is a secondary button. No plan choice is carried from the homepage.

This is not a claim that a wrong payment occurred. No checkout was submitted. It is a mismatch between the action chosen and the next screen.

**Required result:** a monthly visitor must see Monthly as their current choice and retain that choice through the next action. The total, billing period, and primary button must agree. Use the existing purchase owner and interface. Do not introduce a second checkout state owner.

Source owners: homepage plan buttons and the Pro page's purchase form.

### 21. P2 — Free-form input still needs an honest response contract

**Updated source finding.** The blocked-input fault is being repaired. The new field accepts text and says “Ask anything.” But it still maps text to a preset chapter; unmatched text opens the Sam/Acme answer. It does not answer the submitted question.

The visible note explains that this is a tour, which helps. “Ask anything” still overstates what follows. A question about a different subject can produce a detailed answer about Sam with no explicit explanation of the mismatch.

**Required result:** retain the visitor's question and show a clear mapping to a sample feature. For an unsupported request, show “This is a sample of how recall works” before the preset answer. Alternatively, restrict the launcher to named examples. Do not imply a live model call or silently pretend that the preset answer answers arbitrary text.

Source owners: CommandBar, INTENTS, and AskScene. This updated interaction needs a fresh browser review after the owner completes it.

### 22. P2 — Pause does not define what stops

**Source finding, supported by scene changes observed during review.** The control pauses chapter advancement. It does not stop ShotSeq, its 3200 ms image changes, `useCycle` scene remounts, ActScene's auto-approval, or other scene timers. Even a one-image ShotSeq starts its transition again on a timer.

The visitor cannot tell whether Pause means “keep this chapter” or “stop this demonstration.” The first is useful, but it must be named precisely. The approval fault must be fixed independently.

**Required result:** define the pause scope. Give replaying content a clear, stable pause/replay contract. Completed content remains visible long enough to read, and a one-image chapter does not repeatedly wipe in without new information. Hover pause needs the same focus and touch equivalent where it protects reading.

### 23. P2 — There are duplicate spoken labels in specific primitives

**Observed in the browser accessibility tree and confirmed in primitive source.** Free and Monthly actions expose their labels twice because InteractiveHoverButton keeps both visual text layers accessible. TextReveal exposes both its background word and animated word; the browser tree reads “Cloud Cloud AI AI…” through the manifesto.

Do not generalize this fault to every text effect. TextAnimate hides its animated word copies, and the model strip hides repeat copies. Those parts already use a better pattern.

**Required result:** expose one stable accessible label for each animated control or sentence. Hide decorative copies. Keep the selected state and result available separately. Resolve this in the primitive or its thin adapter. A physical screen-reader session remains necessary; this audit inspected the browser accessibility tree.

### 24. P2 — The new tab rows need the rest of the tab contract

**Updated source finding.** The chapter and plan rows use `role="tablist"` and `role="tab"`, but the visible source does not provide a complete tab-to-panel relationship or a focus rule for inactive tabs. AnimatedBackground owns the highlight while separate product state owns selection.

A highlight is not the complete navigation behavior. Previous/next, swipe, tab activation, focus, and autoplay must all update the same selected state and highlight. Otherwise the new control replaces one discovery problem with state disagreement.

**Required result:** use the established tab primitive for tab semantics, or use ordinary labeled buttons if these are not true tabs. For tabs, provide a linked panel, selected state, one tab stop, and arrow-key navigation inside the row. Do not restore the removed window-level arrow handler.

### 25. P2 — The page demonstrates playback more often than interaction

**Design and source finding.** The broad story is useful, but several scenes are timed displays. Fill login and Sign in are spans in BrowserScene, not controls. Model downloads are a timer-driven progress display. Image changes and screenshot controls are playback. Ask shows source names but has no source preview route in the supplied citations.

These can be valid demos. The problem is that their visual form suggests an action the visitor can take. The visitor should not need to tap each apparent control to discover which ones are real.

**Required result:** choose a few complete interaction loops and label the remaining scenes as playback. For example: select a source and inspect a sample excerpt; edit a sample draft and approve it; select a model category and see a stable sample result. Use named library controls and overlays. A smaller number of complete loops is better than many control-shaped props.

### 26. P2 — First-fold clarity and the download path need one main choice

**Design finding.** “Your AI. Your memory” establishes a tone, but it does not quickly explain the practical difference: this is personal AI that runs on hardware the visitor owns. The strongest interaction is the tour launcher; the download action is in the header. The model strip adds capability names before a novice has a clear reason to care.

**Required result:** keep the large headline and short copy. Add one concise, persistent statement of the product and one clear main download action near it. Use a secondary “Explore a sample” action. Match the platform label and requirements to the existing Download page owner. In the final download block, show hardware restrictions before a visitor selects a direct file. Do not add a paragraph of sales copy or repeat the entire platform list in the hero.

The browser extension also needs its release status near the claim. The Download source labels it early access, requiring Desktop, with a community request link. The homepage's unqualified Chrome/Firefox claim does not give those limits.

### 27. P2 — Runtime cost needs a budget, not a promise

**File-size observation only.** During this pass, generated homepage assets were about 755 KB JavaScript and 467 KB CSS before the rebuild; the later files were about 783 KB and 470 KB. These are disk sizes, not compressed transfer sizes, load timings, or frame-rate measurements.

The page combines timers, clip-path wipes, moving edges, blur effects, grids, progress displays, and theme image variants. Several are outside the brand's transform/opacity motion rule. They may still be acceptable if they are measured and serve the product. The audit does not claim that they are slow on a physical device.

**Required result:** set a production load and motion budget. Defer scenes not needed for the first view. Do not eagerly load both theme versions of every preloaded scene without evidence that the cost is acceptable. Stop timers outside the visible section. Later, with user approval, measure the real production page on a mid-range phone. Library reuse alone does not establish runtime quality.

### 28. P2 — Server-rendered fallback is incomplete

**Source finding; JavaScript-disabled rendering was not run.** The server renders the product window before `geo.ready` is true. CSS makes the window opacity 0 until `is-ready` is added. The noscript override targets inline opacity/transform styles, so it does not directly repair that stylesheet rule. Purchase/download buttons also rely on JavaScript navigation, whereas the header links are native anchors.

**Required result:** keep a readable product image and usable main links before hydration and if the client bundle fails. Enhancement can then add the tour. Do not leave the main proof invisible while asking the visitor to trust the page. Confirm this manually when permitted; the source concern is not a measured loading fault.

## Interaction contract for the next iteration

Use this as the design brief. It describes behavior, not custom components.

| Interaction | Immediate feedback | Useful result | State that must remain stable |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hover or focus a chapter | One clear preview and selected/focused distinction | Name and purpose of the chapter | Current chapter until activation |
| Select or swipe to a chapter | Control, title, counter, and frame update together | One readable product result | Selected chapter and completed result |
| Enter a question | Input accepts text; submission is named | Explicit sample mapping or supported result | Submitted question and its explanation |
| Approve, edit, or defer | Press feedback, then the chosen state | Success, editor, or deferred sample | The user's choice; no automatic approval |
| Inspect a source | Visible focus/press feedback | A sample excerpt with source name | Context and a clear route back |
| Change a plan | Highlight, card, amount, and billing label agree | Correct plan handoff | The selected plan at the next page |
| Change theme | Short surface transition | Equally readable content | Scroll position, focus, chapter, and plan |
| Pause playback | Clear paused state | Readable completed content | No timer-driven replacement within that scope |
| Scroll | A clear transition or newly visible content | Progress through the product story | Normal page control; no unrelated key capture |

Do not add animation to every item at the same intensity. Use a distinct response for a distinct action. Ambient motion should create life without drawing attention away from a decision.
